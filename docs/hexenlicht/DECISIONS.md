# Hexenlicht — decision log

Technical decisions made while building Hexenlicht, one line each, with the
story (issue) or PR where they were made. The project-level decisions (engine,
renderer, scope, licensing, DLSS) are in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) §1–§5; how the
code implements these is in [RENDERER.md](RENDERER.md). Add a line when a
story settles something a later session must not undo; mark a line
*superseded* rather than deleting it.

## Process

| # | Decision | Where |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | The owner drives: every story is researched and proposed (one recommendation with reasons, what is left out, deviations from GL), implemented only after approval | PLAN §10 |

| # | Decision | Where |
|---|---|---|

| W4 | Textures are sRGB with GPU mips; the swapchain is UNORM with sRGB color space and the last pass encodes; the `gamma` cvar is applied in the shader; *7.3: HDR swapchains with `vid_hdr` (W5)* | 1.3, 1.5, 1.6 |
| W5 | HDR output (owner, 2026-10-07, from 7.3's proposal): `vid_hdr` (0 by default, archived; Video Modes' HDR row, applied at once): 1 an scRGB swapchain (`R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT`, extended sRGB linear: Windows' composition format), else HDR10 (`A2B10G10R10`, ST 2084, BT.2020); 2 HDR10 only; only when the instance has `VK_EXT_swapchain_colorspace`, DXGI reports the window's monitor in HDR (its G2084 color space) and the surface offers the format, else SDR with the reason (`vk_hdr`). The frame is drawn as in SDR, in the 8-bit color values (the composite with GL's view blend and `gamma`, the 2D: GL's blending, the UI at the paper white), into an `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` frame image that keeps values above 1; a last pass (`hdr_encode.frag`) makes them light times the paper white, at most the peak. SDR still draws into the swapchain image (`main`'s image, no cost). The paper white is Windows' SDR content brightness, the peak the display's DXGI `MaxLuminance` (`r_hdr_white`, `r_hdr_peak` in nits replace them; 200 and 1000 when Windows doesn't say), read at each swapchain creation (Windows' HDR switch and a move to another monitor are seen at the next one). Not a lighting change: light transport, the lights and the exposure are unchanged. Screenshots in HDR are the frame image clipped at 1 (the SDR image), so the test tools keep working; `vk_hdrshot` writes the output in nits (PFM). Left out: Quake II RTX's HDR mapping (W6), wide gamut (the content is BT.709's), HDR metadata (borderless fullscreen, Windows composes), HDR screenshots for players, a paper white row in the menu | 7.3 (#81) |
| W6 | Above SDR white (7.3): the fixed exposure's clip at 1 becomes a shoulder per channel towards the headroom h (peak over paper white), `1 + (h − 1)(1 − e^(−(x − 1)/(h − 1)))` above 1, the identity up to it (slope 1 at 1); per channel as the clip is (a hue-preserving roll-off would turn the ×32 lava a deep red, darker than SDR's orange-white); the auto exposure skips its knee there; FSR runs Quake II RTX's HDR pipelines (its reversible tone map around the passes: the SDR range filters a little differently than in SDR); the debug views and `tm_enable 0` stay clipped at 1. A display mapping, not physically based, as SDR's clip isn't: above white the light shown is the rendered radiance up to the display's peak instead of a flat white. Rejected: Quake II RTX's HDR mapping (exposure 0 at `tm_hdr_peak_nits` 800: the whole calibrated image about 2.7× SDR's 300-nit white), a shoulder starting below 1 (it would change the calibrated range). Measured at 7.3's proposal (8 views, Release, shots 2 stops down): above SDR white only the lava, 0.9–7.4 % of the pixels at meso5, meso8, meso9 (mostly 4× white or more), 0–0.01 % at demo1, the cathedral, castle4, village1 (daylight), tibet1; the owner's display (AW3225QF, 300-nit SDR white, 450-nit peak) has a headroom of 1.5 | 7.3 |
| W7 | The decode (7.3): the frame image's 8-bit values become light by the engine's transfer (R104's 2.2 power, the sRGB curve with `r_srgb 1`): R104's linear light, what an SDR monitor shows in SDR mode. `r_hdr_decode 1` (archived) decodes them by the sRGB curve instead, as Windows shows an SDR program on an HDR desktop (its piecewise sRGB decode lifts the dark tones: 1.15× at a value of 50, 1.6× at 25, 3.8× at 10; equal from about 128): the SDR look on the owner's HDR desktop, on which every look so far was judged. Above 1 (the shoulder) both decode by the engine's transfer, which encoded it, so both reach the peak alike. Default 0 (owner, 2026-10-07, after looking at both: "Decode 0 looks right") | 7.3 |
| W8 | Checked (TESTING.md "HDR output (7.3)", `hdr_run.ps1`; the owner's AW3225QF: paper white 300 nits, peak 450): Debug validation 0/0 with `vid_hdr` 0, 1 and 2 switched live, FSR (both passes, EASU alone, RCAS alone after TAAU), the auto exposure, the debug views, the main menu, `vk_reload_shaders`, `vid_restart`, `resize_test.ps1`'s resize, maximize, restore and too small; Release with DLSS SR and RR (`-validation`) 0/0. The albedo view in SDR and HDR equal but for 1 of 255 in about 1.5 % of the values (the half floats' rounding); `vk_hdrshot`'s output against the same frame's screenshot below white within 0.55 of 255 of 300 × value^2.2 nits (scRGB; HDR10 within 1–2 at demo1 and up to 12 in the saturated lava's near-black channels: its 10-bit PQ through BT.2020 and back, in the check), of the sRGB curve with `r_hdr_decode 1`, of 200 nits with `r_hdr_white 200`; above white (meso8's lava) 299–450 nits, none above the peak (scRGB). Cost not measured cleanly: the owner's texture pack job (ComfyUI) kept the GPU 35–90 % busy the whole evening; under that load the encode pass (`HDR output`) took 0.05–0.24 ms at 2560x1440 (Release), and the composite and 2D into the half-float image showed no consistent difference against SDR's; to be re-measured with `hdr_run.ps1 -Cost` on an idle GPU. Not exercised: Alt-Tab and Windows' HDR switch while running, a second monitor, another vendor's driver | 7.3 |

## Scene and geometry

| # | Decision | Where |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | GPU data follows Quake II RTX's formats (`VboPrimitive`, material IDs and table, `ModelInstance`, AS masks, the instanced buffer, model groups incl. the viewer weapon, the effects TLAS), so its shaders can be imported | 2.1–2.8 |
| G2 | The 3D renderer reads only `r_scene` (filled by `R_RenderView`); entities are not culled to the view | 1.7 |
| G3 | A cluster is a vis leaf (leaf number - 1); the PVS is made symmetric and connected across water/slime/translucent (not lava) | 2.1, 2.2 |
| G4 | World triangles facing into solid (qbsp leftovers) are dropped | 2.1 |
| G5 | Frame blending (`r_lerpmodels 1`, archived, default) over each entity's own measured interval (Hexen II animates at 20 or 10 Hz); 0 = GL's poses | 2.4a (#26) |
| G6 | Movement blending for stepping monsters (`r_lerpmove 1`, archived, default), QuakeSpasm's but over the interval between the monster's last two moves; a move before the glide ends glides on from the shown position (QuakeSpasm jumps first); attached lights/trails/sounds stay at the server position; 0 = GL's steps | 2.9 (#108, PR #114) |
| G7 | Translated player skins are translated in 8 bits and keep their cutouts (GL loses the Demoness's); `TEX_SPECIAL_TRANS` alpha is stored as opacity | 2.4b (#105) |
| G8 | The first-person weapon: GL's fov compensation above 90 (no separate gun FOV), its own model group and BLAS, traced first from GL's 4-unit near plane so it stays in front of walls | 2.8 (#30, PR #113) |
| G9 | `cl.light_level` (sent to the server: monster awareness, Assassin cloak) is computed as GL's `R_DrawViewModel` does | 2.8 |
| G10 | Particles are GL's camera-facing triangles with its dot texture and colors; sprites all five orientation types, unlit; `SPR_FACING_UPRIGHT` uses the sprite's own direction (GL's stale `modelorg`; unused by the game); *scaled by the exposure since 3.7 (R41)* | 2.5 (#27, PR #112) |
| G11 | Effects live in a second, effects-only TLAS and never block rays through the main TLAS | 2.5 |
| G12 | Translucent models and surfaces are opaque in the debug view until 6.4 (the main TLAS is force-opaque except cutouts, so water and translucent surfaces hide the effects behind them); a translucent weapon will need effects blended behind it; *since 3.5b translucent surfaces and models are seen through, effects behind them show (R33); water stays opaque (R31); since 6.4 blended as GL per type (X15–X17); since 6.5 water is seen through as physical water (X21)* | 2.4b, 2.8 |
| G13 | The sunstaff beam report (2.10) was not a bug: the engines' `screenshot` capture different frames; compare paused | 2.10 (#111, PR #115) |
| G14 | Model shake is mostly the MDL's rounding (8.1's measurement): one byte per axis on one grid per model (0.3 units at the median, 0.9 on the bosses), each vertex rounded on its own in every frame, and the 162 table normals (`anorms.h`) the shading uses. The smoothing (owner, 2026-10-08, for E8): per sequence (frames named alike but for a trailing number; a frame group's subframes), each vertex's path on each axis the smoothest one (least squared second differences) within 0.49 grid steps of its byte, so rounding gives back the original (checked: 0 of 14.25 million samples differ); a loop (a frame group, or the last pose within 1.5× the mean frame-to-frame distance of the first) smoothed across the wrap. Normals rebuilt from the smoothed shape as the original game's tables were made (each face's unit normal counts the same: its median model 5.7 degrees from the table, the table's spacing; area-weighted 10.3 over all models), with three rules found in 8.1, each decided so a face never drops in and out between frames: a face under a quarter of a grid cell counts by its area (collapsed parts), of faces on the same three vertices (back-to-back: the flag, the seaweed, plants) the one that agrees with the table over all poses, and a vertex whose faces weigh less than a full face takes the rest from its table normal (the medusa's severed head). UV seams (found in 8.1's code review): the original game's models (IDPO) duplicate the vertices along every UV seam (the imp 117 of 270) and each duplicate's table normal is its own side's, so today's shading creases at the seams; Portal of Praevus' models (RAPO, pak3) welded those duplicates and kept one side's table normal (checked: every vertex of its imp and golem carries one pak1 duplicate's), so rebuilt normals differ from their tables by 32 degrees (median model) whatever is chosen. `-Weld` (vertices at the same place in every pose share one normal, but not a card's two sides: vertices whose faces lie on each other only once welded, or whose table normals point against each other, mean dot under −0.5; else the plants' and the scarab's wings' sides cancel, found in the code review) makes both games smooth across seams, 27–34 degrees from today's look (median model per pak), about as steady (normal jerk 3.19 against 3.24; worse than the table also on `axblade`, `stlghtng`, `hamthrow`, `funnal`): a look for the owner to choose in 8.2. Measured (TESTING.md "Model smoothing (8.1)"): idle animations, under a grid step per frame, rigid again (edges 0.84 → 0.27 of rounding, normal jerk 2.5 → 0.23 degrees, the imp's idle 3.6 → 0.08); fast ones keep their positions (1.12 → 1.01) and get steadier normals (7.2 → 3.6). Left out: rigid-bone fitting (changes the authored animation), curves through more than two frames | 8.1 (#203) |
